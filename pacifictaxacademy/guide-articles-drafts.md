# How to Register with CTEC: A Step-by-Step Guide for California Tax Preparers

*From your first application to your CRTP certificate, here's the order CTEC lays out.*

**SEO title tag:** How to Register with CTEC: A Step-by-Step Guide
**Meta description:** The CTEC registration steps for California tax preparers: online application, Live Scan, 60-hour course, PTIN, $5,000 bond, and fees.

**Direct answer:** To register with CTEC, you submit an online application with a $102 fee, pass a Live Scan background check, complete a 60-hour qualifying course from a CTEC-approved provider, get an IRS PTIN and a $5,000 surety bond, then finish registration and pay the $35 annual registration fee (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/; https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

## Who needs to register with CTEC

If you prepare tax returns for a fee in California, you must register with CTEC unless you're exempt (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

The exempt groups are California CPAs, active members of the State Bar of California, and IRS enrolled agents (https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp; https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

This applies even if you only prepare federal returns. CTEC says federal-only preparers must still register if they don't meet an exemption (https://ctec.org/faqs/).

Once you finish, you're a CTEC Registered Tax Preparer, or CRTP (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

## What you'll need before you start

Here's the checklist CTEC gives new applicants (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/):

| Requirement | Details | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Online application | $100 application fee + $2 processing fee, nonrefundable | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |
| Live Scan background check | Fingerprints sent to the California DOJ | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |
| 60-hour Qualifying Education (QE) | From a CTEC-approved provider | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |
| IRS PTIN | Preparer Tax Identification Number | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |
| $5,000 surety bond | Uploaded to your CTEC account | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |
| Registration fee | $35 annual, paid after requirements are approved | https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/ |

For the application itself, have your full Social Security number, your name as it appears on your card, your address, contact information, and a current email address ready (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

## Step-by-step: registering with CTEC

1. **Submit the New Preparer Application.** You'll create a user ID and password, complete a background questionnaire, and pay the $102 fee ($100 + $2 processing) (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
2. **Complete Live Scan.** Log in to your account to download the Live Scan form, then take it to a Live Scan location. The operator sends your fingerprints to the California Department of Justice. Don't mail the form to CTEC (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
3. **Finish your 60-hour qualifying course.** It must come from a CTEC-approved provider. CTEC's registration webinar describes the course as 43 hours of federal taxation, 15 hours of California taxation, and 2 hours of ethics, with a passing score of at least 70% on each topic (https://www.ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/CTEC-Registration-Requirements.pdf).
4. **Get your PTIN from the IRS.** CTEC requires a valid PTIN and points you to IRS.gov (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
5. **Buy a $5,000 tax preparer surety bond** from a surety company and upload a copy to your CTEC account (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
6. **Wait for CTEC's approval email.** If CTEC asks for more information, respond. Not responding "may result in a proposed denial" (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
7. **Complete registration and pay the $35 annual fee** through your online account (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

You can take the qualifying course before you apply. Just know that if you take the course and then fail the background check, you can't register (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

## Timelines and deadlines to keep in mind

- **Live Scan results** typically take 2 to 8 weeks to reach CTEC from the DOJ, followed by CTEC's review (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
- **Your qualifying course** is valid for up to 18 months from completion. Miss that window and you must retake the course (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/; https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).
- **Your application and Live Scan results** are each valid for up to 3 years from submission (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
- **The registration cycle** runs November 1 to October 31 (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
- **Your certificate** is mailed within 4 to 6 weeks after approval and payment (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
- **New for 2027:** Starting January 1, 2027, every QE final exam must be preceded by a penalty-of-perjury statement that you completed the course without help from others (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Provider-July-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

One rule matters more than the rest: new applicants can't prepare returns for clients until registration is complete and approved (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

## After you're registered: renewing each year

Each year you'll need 20 hours of continuing education: 10 hours of federal tax law, 3 hours of federal tax law updates, 2 hours of ethics, and 5 hours of California tax law (https://ctec.org/media-room/crtp-renewal-requirements-what-registered-tax-preparers-need-to-know/).

Renewal opens August 1 and is due October 31. Late renewals are accepted through January 15. After that, your registration expires and you start over with a new application (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

CE you took before your registration was approved doesn't count toward renewal (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Provider-July-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

## FAQ

**Can I prepare returns while my CTEC application is pending?**
No. CTEC says new applicants aren't permitted to prepare tax returns for clients until registration is complete and approved (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

**What happens if I prepare returns without registering?**
CTEC reports that the FTB issues a $2,500 first failure-to-register penalty, waived if you show proof of a valid credential within 90 days, and a $5,000 penalty for each later failure (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Preparer-August-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

**I used to be an EA or CPA. Do I need the full 60 hours?**
Maybe not. Certain experienced preparers, including recently lapsed CPAs, attorneys, and EAs, can take a 15-hour California tax law course instead, but they still meet every other registration requirement (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Experience-in-Lieu.pdf).

**How long does the whole process take?**
CTEC doesn't publish a single total. Live Scan alone typically takes 2 to 8 weeks to reach CTEC, and CTEC says to contact them if you're still pending after 8 weeks (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

**Course suggestion:** New to CTEC? Start with Pacific Tax Academy's [60-Hour Qualifying Course](/60-hour-qualifying-course/) ($149.95), then move to the [20-Hour Renewal](/20-hour-ctec-renewal/) after you're registered.

# How to Get a PTIN from the IRS as a California Tax Preparer

*Your PTIN is one of the requirements for CTEC registration. Here's how to apply and keep it current.*

**SEO title tag:** How to Get a PTIN from the IRS (2027 Guide)
**Meta description:** How California tax preparers apply for or renew an IRS PTIN: what you need, the $18.75 fee, online vs. Form W-12, and how it fits CTEC registration.

**Direct answer:** You get a PTIN by creating an account in the IRS online PTIN system, answering the application questions, and paying the $18.75 nonrefundable fee; most first-time applicants finish online in about 15 minutes (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

## What a PTIN is and who needs one

A PTIN is a Preparer Tax Identification Number. The IRS says anyone who prepares or assists in preparing federal tax returns for compensation must have a valid 2027 PTIN before preparing returns (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

Enrolled agents need one too (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

In California, a PTIN is also one of CTEC's registration requirements. You can't become a CTEC Registered Tax Preparer without one (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

A PTIN belongs to you as an individual, not to the firm you work for (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-renewal-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started).

## What to have ready before you apply

The IRS lists these items on its PTIN application checklist (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-application-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started):

- [ ] Your Social Security number
- [ ] Personal information: name, mailing address, and date of birth
- [ ] Business information: name, mailing address, and phone number
- [ ] Your previous year's individual tax return (name, address, and filing status), used to verify your identity
- [ ] Explanations of any felony convictions, if applicable
- [ ] Explanations of any problems with your U.S. individual or business tax obligations, if applicable
- [ ] A credit, debit, or ATM card for the $18.75 user fee
- [ ] Any U.S.-based professional credential details (certification number, jurisdiction, expiration date), if applicable

If you filed your most recent return within the past 8 weeks, the IRS says to use the prior year's return for identity verification (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-application-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started).

Individuals generally must provide a Social Security number. People with an ITIN aren't eligible for a PTIN unless they're foreign persons with a permanent non-U.S. address (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-do-i-need-a-ptin).

## Applying online, step by step

1. **Go to the PTIN system** at https://rpr.irs.gov (linked from https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).
2. **Select "Create an Account"** (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-application-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started). [verify: current sign-in method, e.g., ID.me or Login.gov; the IRS PTIN page links help pages for both]
3. **Verify your personal information and answer the application questions** (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).
4. **Pay the $18.75 fee** by credit/debit/ATM card or eCheck. It's nonrefundable and nontransferable (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).
5. **Save your PTIN.** You'll enter it on your CTEC application and give it to your education providers (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

The IRS says most first-time applicants can finish online in about 15 minutes (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

## Applying on paper with Form W-12

If you'd rather not apply online, you can file Form W-12 (https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/fw12.pdf) and mail it with the $18.75 fee to:

IRS Tax Pro PTIN Processing Center
PO Box 380638
San Antonio, TX 78268

(https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers)

Plan ahead. Paper applications take about 6 weeks to process (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

## Renewing your PTIN every year

PTINs must be renewed each year. The IRS says current PTINs expire December 31, 2026 (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminder-tax-professionals-should-renew-their-ptins-for-2027).

To renew, sign in to your PTIN account and select "Renew my PTIN." Online renewal generally takes about 15 minutes, and the fee is $18.75 (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminder-tax-professionals-should-renew-their-ptins-for-2027).

Keep your personal email current in your PTIN account, since most PTIN correspondence comes through secure messaging there (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-renewal-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started).

## How your PTIN fits into CTEC registration

For California preparers, your PTIN does double duty:

- **CTEC registration:** A valid PTIN is required for new applicants (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/), and a current PTIN is required at each renewal (https://ctec.org/media-room/crtp-renewal-requirements-what-registered-tax-preparers-need-to-know/).
- **Education credit reporting:** CTEC asks you to give your education providers both your CTEC ID and your PTIN so credits get reported to the IRS (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

A PTIN by itself doesn't make you a CTEC registrant. In California, you still need the qualifying course, Live Scan, bond, and CTEC approval unless you're exempt (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

## FAQ

**How much does a PTIN cost?**
$18.75, for both new applications and renewals. The fee is nonrefundable (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

**Should I get my PTIN before or after the 60-hour course?**
CTEC lists the course, PTIN, and bond as separate steps, and you need all three done before you complete online registration (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

**Can a felony conviction affect my PTIN?**
The IRS says felony convictions "may affect your ability to obtain a PTIN," and you'll be asked to explain any (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-application-checklist-what-you-need-to-get-started).

**Does my PTIN replace CTEC registration?**
No. If you prepare returns for a fee in California and aren't exempt, you still need to register with CTEC (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

**Course suggestion:** Once your PTIN is in hand, the [60-Hour Qualifying Course](/60-hour-qualifying-course/) covers the education piece of CTEC registration.

# The $5,000 Tax Preparer Bond for CTEC Registration, Explained

*What California law requires, what the bond covers, and how to keep it current with CTEC.*

**SEO title tag:** The $5,000 CTEC Tax Preparer Bond Explained
**Meta description:** California's $5,000 tax preparer bond for CTEC: what the law requires, what the bond covers, how to upload it, and how to keep it current.

**Direct answer:** Every CTEC Registered Tax Preparer must maintain a $5,000 surety bond issued by a surety company admitted to do business in California, and $5,000 is the bond's coverage amount, not what you pay for it (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/; https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/preparer-bonding-requirements/).

## What the tax preparer bond is (and isn't)

The bond protects taxpayers. CTEC describes it as a $5,000 surety bond "to protect taxpayers from fraud" (https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp).

It's not insurance for you, and it's not a fee paid to CTEC. You buy it from a surety company (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

The $5,000 figure is coverage. CTEC is clear: "This is the bond's coverage amount, not the price of buying the bond" (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/preparer-bonding-requirements/). [verify: current premium ranges from California-admitted sureties]

CTEC notes that CRTPs are the only tax professionals in California required to carry this bond (https://ctec.org/faqs/).

## What California law requires

The bond comes from California Business and Professions Code section 22250.1, which CTEC reproduces on its legislative authority page (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/). [verify: confirm current text on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov, which blocked automated access]

Key points from the statute:

- A tax preparer must maintain a bond "issued by a surety company admitted to do business in this state."
- The bond is required "for each individual preparing tax returns for another person."
- Before a bond is issued, the surety must receive proof that the individual is at least 18.
- The bond is payable to the people of California, for the benefit of anyone damaged by the preparer's fraud, dishonesty, misstatement, or similar conduct.
- You can't substitute a cash deposit for the bond.
- You can't conduct business without a current bond.

(All from https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/)

## What the bond does and doesn't cover

| | Covered? | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Clients harmed by a preparer's fraud, dishonesty, or misstatement | Yes | https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/ |
| Total surety liability per preparer | Capped at $5,000, regardless of number of claims or years in force | https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/ |
| Civil penalties, fines, attorneys' fees | No | https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/ |
| Your own business losses | Not addressed by the statute [verify with your surety] | — |

If you run a firm with several preparers, each individual needs bond coverage, and the statute caps the total bond required for any single tax preparer at $125,000 (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

## How to get a bond and upload it to CTEC

1. **Shop for a $5,000 tax preparer surety bond** from a surety company admitted in California (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/). [verify: how to confirm a surety's admitted status with the California Department of Insurance]
2. **Give the surety proof you're at least 18** (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).
3. **Check the effective date.** CTEC's system won't accept a bond with a future effective date beyond 10 days. It must be dated in the past or within the next 10 days (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/; https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp).
4. **Upload a copy to your CTEC account** as part of registration (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

The bond is one of the last pieces of registration. CTEC lists it after the 60-hour course and PTIN, before online registration and payment (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

## Keeping your bond current

Your bond has to stay active for as long as you're registered:

- **At renewal:** CTEC verifies your bond information. If it's expired, you'll be prompted to upload a new one (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Registration-Renewal-Process-updated.pdf).
- **Changes:** The statute requires a bond amendment within 30 days of any change in bond information (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).
- **Cancellation:** The surety must send written notice to you and CTEC 30 days before cancelling. If a replacement isn't in place by then, you must stop doing business (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

## Bond claims and telling your clients

CRTPs must give clients the surety company name and bond number (https://ctec.org/faqs/).

The statute also requires written notice to clients, before you provide services, that includes evidence of bond compliance and the bond number (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

Don't advertise your bond as a state license or endorsement. That's a violation under section 22253 (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

If a claim is paid against your bond, you must report it to CTEC through your account. CTEC publishes reported paid claims on its Preparer Paid Bond Claims page (https://ctec.org/faqs/). [verify: exact reporting steps in the CTEC account, "Add New Paid Bond Claim"]

## FAQ

**Is the bond the same as errors and omissions insurance?**
No. The bond protects taxpayers harmed by fraud, dishonesty, or misstatement, and it doesn't pay civil penalties, fines, or attorneys' fees (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/). [verify: E&O coverage terms with an insurer]

**Do CPAs, attorneys, and EAs need this bond?**
They're exempt from CTEC registration (https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp), and CTEC says CRTPs are the only California tax professionals required to carry the bond (https://ctec.org/faqs/).

**Can I buy the bond before Live Scan clears?**
You can buy it any time, but the effective date can't be more than 10 days in the future when you upload it (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/). [verify: whether the CTEC upload option appears only after Live Scan approval]

**How much does the bond cost?**
CTEC doesn't publish a price. The $5,000 is coverage, not cost (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/preparer-bonding-requirements/).

**Course suggestion:** The bond is one of several CTEC requirements. The [60-Hour Qualifying Course](/60-hour-qualifying-course/) handles the education requirement.

# Live Scan Fingerprinting for California Tax Preparers: What CTEC Requires

*Yes, CTEC requires it. Here's when it happens, how it works, and what to expect.*

**SEO title tag:** Live Scan for CTEC Tax Preparers in California
**Meta description:** CTEC requires new California tax preparers to pass a Live Scan background check. When to do it, how it works, out-of-state options, and wait times.

**Direct answer:** Yes, CTEC requires every new applicant to submit fingerprints through Live Scan and pass a California Department of Justice background check before registration is approved (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/; https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

## Does CTEC require Live Scan?

It does. CTEC lists "Completion and passing of a Live Scan background check" as a requirement for new preparers (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

The requirement is in state law. Business and Professions Code section 22253.5 says that beginning July 1, 2020, "as a condition of registration, the council shall require the applicant to submit fingerprint images" (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

Your fingerprints go to the DOJ, which checks for records of state- and federal-level convictions (https://ctec.org/about-us/legislative-authority/).

This applies to new applicants. It also applies if your registration expired and you have to reapply as a new preparer (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Provider-July-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

## When Live Scan happens in the process

Live Scan is step 2 of CTEC's application process. You submit the online application and pay the $102 fee first. Then the Live Scan form becomes available in your account (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

Don't get the form anywhere else. CTEC has told education providers to stop handing out Request for Live Scan Service forms, because the wrong form can delay your application (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Provider-July-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

You can take the 60-hour qualifying course before your background check clears. But if you fail the background check, you can't register (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

## Step-by-step: Live Scan in California

1. **Submit your CTEC New Preparer Application** and pay the $102 fee (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
2. **Log in and download the Live Scan Request Form** from your application page (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).
3. **Find a Live Scan location** using the California DOJ's county search (https://oag.ca.gov/fingerprints/locations).
4. **Call ahead.** The DOJ suggests contacting providers in advance to confirm hours and fees (https://oag.ca.gov/fingerprints/locations).
5. **Bring valid photo ID.** Expired IDs aren't accepted (https://oag.ca.gov/fingerprints/locations).
6. **Keep your copy.** The operator sends your fingerprints to the DOJ and returns a copy to you. Don't send it to CTEC (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

## If you're applying from outside California

You'll use a mail-in process instead (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/):

- [ ] Form BCIA 8016, Request for Live Scan Service
- [ ] FD-258 fingerprint hard cards (the DOJ recommends two sets; CTEC doesn't provide them)
- [ ] A $32 check or money order payable to CA DOJ
- [ ] Mail to the DOJ, not CTEC: California Department of Justice, Bureau of Criminal Information and Analysis, Attn: Pre-Scan Unit, P.O. Box 903417, Sacramento, CA 94203-4170

## What you need to disclose

Questions about your history should go to CTEC directly, not your course provider (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Provider-July-2026-Newsletter.pdf). CTEC's number is (877) 850-2832 (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

## What happens after your Live Scan

Your application stays "Pending" until CTEC receives your Live Scan report and you've finished the 60-hour course (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

If CTEC needs more information, it will email you. If you don't respond, that "may result in a proposed denial." If you're denied, CTEC notifies you by email and mail with the reason and the appeal process (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

Still pending after 8 weeks? CTEC asks you to call or email (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/new-application/).

# CTEC vs. IRS AFSP vs. Enrolled Agent: Which Credential Do You Need?

*How California's CTEC registration compares with the IRS Annual Filing Season Program and enrolled agent status.*

**SEO title tag:** CTEC vs AFSP vs Enrolled Agent: Which Do You Need?
**Meta description:** Comparing CTEC registration, the IRS AFSP, and enrolled agent status for California tax preparers: requirements, hours, fees, and representation rights.

**Direct answer:** If you prepare returns for a fee in California and you're not a CPA, attorney, or enrolled agent, you need CTEC registration; the AFSP is an optional IRS program you can add on top, and becoming an enrolled agent is a separate federal route that exempts you from CTEC (https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp; https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-annual-filing-season-program).

## The short version

- **CTEC (CRTP):** A California requirement. Mandatory for non-exempt preparers who prepare returns for a fee in the state (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).
- **AFSP:** A voluntary IRS program for non-credentialed preparers that comes with limited representation rights (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/annual-filing-season-program; https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-annual-filing-season-program).
- **Enrolled agent (EA):** A federal credential with unlimited practice rights before the IRS. EAs are exempt from CTEC registration (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/enrolled-agent-information; https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp).

All three need a PTIN (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/ptin-requirements-for-tax-return-preparers).

## CTEC registration (CRTP)

CTEC registration is what lets you legally prepare returns for a fee in California if you're not exempt (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).

To register, you need (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/):

- A 60-hour qualifying course from a CTEC-approved provider
- A passed Live Scan background check
- An IRS PTIN
- A $5,000 surety bond
- A $102 application fee and $35 annual registration fee

To stay registered, you complete 20 hours of CE each year (10 federal tax law, 3 federal tax law updates, 2 ethics, 5 California tax law) and renew by October 31 (https://ctec.org/media-room/crtp-renewal-requirements-what-registered-tax-preparers-need-to-know/).

Skipping it has consequences. CTEC reports that the FTB issues a $2,500 first failure-to-register penalty, and $5,000 for each later failure (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Preparer-August-2026-Newsletter.pdf).

## IRS Annual Filing Season Program (AFSP)

The AFSP is voluntary (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-annual-filing-season-program). The IRS points non-credentialed preparers to it (https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-reminder-tax-professionals-should-renew-their-ptins-for-2027).

Most participants need 18 hours of CE, including a six-hour federal tax law refresher course with a test, plus a renewed PTIN and consent to Circular 230 obligations (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/annual-filing-season-program).

**Here's the good news for California:** CTEC registrants are exempt from the refresher course. They need 15 CE hours (10 federal tax law, 3 federal tax law updates, 2 ethics) by December 31 (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-annual-filing-season-program).

Those 15 hours overlap with CTEC's 20-hour renewal. CTEC says CRTPs are generally exempt from the refresher if they complete their 20 CE hours and renew by October 31 (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

What you get (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/annual-filing-season-program):

- An AFSP Record of Completion
- A listing in the IRS public database of return preparers
- Limited representation rights for returns you prepared and signed, before certain IRS employees, including the Taxpayer Advocate Service

## Enrolled agent (EA)

The IRS calls the EA "the highest credential the IRS awards" (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/enrolled-agent-information).

To become one (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/become-an-enrolled-agent):

1. Get a PTIN.
2. Pass all three parts of the Special Enrollment Examination (SEE) within three years. The fee is $317 per part (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/enrolled-agents-frequently-asked-questions).
3. Apply with Form 23 and pay the $140 enrollment fee (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/applying-for-enrollment-to-practice-before-the-irs).
4. Pass a suitability check, including tax compliance and a criminal background check.

EAs complete 72 CE hours every three years, with a minimum of 16 hours per year, including 2 hours of ethics (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/maintain-your-enrolled-agent-status).

## Which path fits you?

- **You're starting out in California:** Register with CTEC first. It's the legal baseline for paid, non-exempt preparers here.
- **You're already a CRTP:** The AFSP is a natural add-on, since your CTEC CE can count toward it. Renew with CTEC by October 31 to avoid AFSP discrepancies (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).
- **You want to represent clients in audits, collections, or appeals:** Look at the EA route (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/enrolled-agents/enrolled-agent-information).

Many California preparers start as CRTPs and work toward the EA later. If you once held EA status and let it lapse, you may qualify for a 15-hour California course instead of the full 60 hours (https://ctec.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Experience-in-Lieu.pdf).

## FAQ

**Does the AFSP replace CTEC registration in California?**
No. The AFSP is voluntary, and CTEC's exemptions cover CPAs, attorneys, and EAs, not AFSP participants (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/frequently-asked-questions-annual-filing-season-program; https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp).

**If I become an EA, do I still need CTEC?**
No. Enrolled agents are exempt from CTEC registration (https://ctec.org/what-is-crtp).

**Can my CTEC hours count toward the AFSP?**
Generally, yes. Give your provider your CTEC ID and PTIN so hours get reported to both (https://ctec.org/taxpreparers/).

**Does a PTIN alone let me prepare returns in California?**
The IRS says anyone with a PTIN can prepare returns for compensation (https://www.irs.gov/tax-professionals/annual-filing-season-program), but California also requires CTEC registration unless you're exempt (https://ctec.org/media-room/how-to-become-a-tax-preparer-in-california/).
